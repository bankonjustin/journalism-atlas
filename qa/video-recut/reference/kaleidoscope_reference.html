Nodes and Networks Kaleidoscope
Nodes and Networks · visual studies · draft for review

Turn the lens.

The same 169 organizations, seen six ways. From a distance the field looks like the trends everyone already knows. Turn to governance and it snaps into nine patterns that behave differently. That turn is the kaleidoscope.

What the sketch above is

A real chart that happens to look like a kaleidoscope

Each mark is one organization from the Figure 5 dataset (v22, 169 rows: 80 below $3.5M, 34 between $3.5M and $10M, 55 above $10M). Nine wedges are the nine cohorts. The two rings are the paper's own thresholds, $3.5M and $10M, so distance from the center means revenue band. Colour and shape follow the edition's three groups and nothing else.

The kaleidoscope does not have to be a decoration laid over the data. The turning is the argument. The first lens pools everything into one neutral spiral, which is the "things are as you knew them" view. Every later lens is a different way of sorting the same marks, and the patterns tighten each time.

Sketch only. Position inside a ring uses internal revenue estimates; 16 of the 169 rows carry a release flag other than INCLUDE (proxies, historical finals, unverified). The public build should place marks by band and rank, never by dollar value. Hover or tap a mark for its name.

Two directions, one instrument

What we could build

A. The turn (hero + social)

A 12 to 15 second sequence: pooled spiral, mirrored into a symmetric mandala, then the mirror drops away and the marks fly to their cohort wedges. Ends on the rosette with the two rings. Exported as video and GIF for launch posts, and as the edition's cover moment (plays once, final state under reduced motion).

B. The 169, moving

An explorable scene with the same marks and five lenses. Readers click a lens and watch which marks travel together. Where they stay clustered, governance predicts. Where they scatter, it does not. Each mark opens a small card: descriptor, cohort, band, mechanism, founding year.

C. The carousel

Eleven 4:5 cards drawn from the same renderer: a cover, the turn, one card per cohort with its wedge lit and its band split, and a close on the six mechanisms. One data file, three outputs, so the numbers can never disagree with the paper.

Sketch B · motion over time

Founded: how the nodes arrived

The nodes moving works best when the motion means time. Play it and the field fills in by founding year. Legacy and public media arrive across a century. Creator brands, co-ops and civic outlets arrive almost all at once, after 2008. Closed organizations finish as hollow marks.

The year axis is compressed before 1990 so the last three decades get most of the width. One row has no founding year and is left out of this view.

Node anatomy

What each mark could carry

Colours of different sizes, with words and tags about audience, mission and context. The dataset supports some of that well and some of it thinly, so the encoding leans on the strong fields.

Comparative comps

Work that already does a piece of this

None of these is the whole idea. Each one solves one part of it: regrouping the same marks, giving each mark a face, making motion carry meaning, or making a dataset feel handmade.

The New York Times, 2012 · Shan Carter and colleagues

Four Ways to Slice Obama's 2013 Budget Proposal

One set of bubbles, one per budget item, that fly into new groupings when you change the slice.

Take the core mechanic for direction B: identical marks, re-sorted by lens, with motion that lets readers track individuals across views.

OECD, 2011 · Moritz Stefaner with Raureif

OECD Better Life Index

Each country is a flower whose petals are dimensions of well-being. Readers re-weight the dimensions and the field reorders.

Take the idea that a mark can be a small glyph with several attributes, and that the reader's choice of lens is the interaction.

Periscopic, 2013

U.S. Gun Deaths: Stolen Years

Each death is one arc that animates through the years lost. The motion is the meaning, not ornament.

Take the rule for the 169: every movement should encode something real. Here that is a lens change or the passage of time.

The New York Times, 2014 · The Upshot

How the Recession Reshaped the Economy, in 255 Charts

Hundreds of industries, each a line, sorted into a single scatter you can read as patterns and then drill into one at a time.

Take the zoom from field to individual: macro pattern first, then each node is findable and has a name.

Federica Fragapane · ongoing practice

The living languages of data visualization

Data drawn as organic, crafted shapes that still read as precise encodings.

Take permission for the hero to be beautiful, as long as every shape can be decoded. Useful for James's cover pass.

Nadieh Bremer and Shirley Wu · Data Sketches

Data Sketches

A body of radial, hand-built visualizations, documented from sketch to code.

Take the radial grammar and the process: rings that mean something, wedges that mean something, labels set around the edge.

The one copy change this needs

Scope the $10M claim to the organizations that crossed it

Coding all 55 organizations above $10M showed that nine fit none of the six mechanisms. Eight of those nine were born above the line or inherited the scale (Nexstar, TelevisaUnivision, Gannett, CNN, the Washington Post, the LA Times, CPB, Consumer Reports). They never crossed it, so the six do not apply to them. Scoping the sentence keeps the finding and makes it exact.

Current: "No organization in this dataset sits above $10 million in revenue without having done one of those six things."

Proposed: "Of the 30 organizations in this dataset that crossed $10 million, 29 did it through one of those six. The exception, Joe Rogan's show, crossed on an exclusive platform licensing deal. The other 25 above the line were already there at launch or inherited their scale."

Figure 5's counts do not change (80 / 34 / 55, total 169). CPB and Consumer Reports stay in the set with a note. Iowa Public Radio moves to "inherited" (formed in 2004 by merging three university stations). Row-by-row coding: MECHANISM-CODING-RESOLVED-2026-10-23.csv.

Open for discussion

What we would like your read on

  1. The sentence above. It is locked copy in the ASU edition, so it needs Andy's OK in both versions.
  2. Bundles. No organization that crossed $10M did it with a bundle. The paper's bundle examples (the Times, the Journal) were already above the line. Worth a clause saying bundles are built after arrival?
  3. Two metaphors. The edition speaks transit map: stations, lines, "back to the map". The kaleidoscope sits beside it as the motion while the map stays the navigation. The nine wedges are the nine stations seen from above.
  4. Where it lives. The social pack (the turn as video, the 12-card carousel) can ship at launch without touching the story page. The explorer could follow as a seventh embed. For Carrie: the same renderer can export any frame at ASU's sizes, so both editions show the same marks.
  5. Precision and motion. Public marks place by band and rank, never dollars. The turn plays once on the page and loops only in social video. The mirror effect is labelled as illustration.

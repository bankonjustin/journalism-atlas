# Atlas Brand Guide — v5

*The Independent Journalism Atlas · Owner: James Bareham, Creative Director · October 27, 2026*

The team's complete brand reference for the website, decks, style guides, social and editorial graphics, and other brand materials. Implementation values live in DESIGN-TOKENS-v16.md. Logos and print color specs live in Atlas-Style_Guide_October_2026_v1.pdf.

**What changed in v5**

v5 consolidates v4; it changes no colors, sizes or components. It locks the design rules, limits the brand to three typefaces, and says exactly where each one goes.

- **Design rules are locked.** Justin makes product, structure, copy and build decisions. Colors, type and the other design values come only from DESIGN-TOKENS-v16.md and change only through a new version approved by James. See Section 8.
- **Three typefaces, nothing else:** Inter, Merriweather and Source Code Pro (plus Material Symbols Outlined for icons). Every other font is off-brand on journalismatlas.com and in Atlas materials.
- **Headings are always Inter.** Merriweather is body prose in editorial articles (essays and long-form reports) only, never headlines, subheads, numerals or pull quotes.
- **No text below 12px, anywhere,** including charts and legends. 12px is for Source Code Pro tags only; everything else is 13px or larger.
- **One header and one footer across the site,** with the footer's type specified.
- **Co-branded reports may carry partner design,** by named exception. Nodes and Networks is the first.

**Carried over from v4** (still in force)
- **The website moves to the Neutral gray scale.** The Cool scale is kept for graphics: infographics, charts, data visualizations and editorial illustrations. An embedded graphic counts as its own surface.
- **Green means interaction.** Acid Green marks hover, press and selected states, and may be used for eyebrow labels on dark surfaces. Lime Green is the keyboard focus ring on dark surfaces only.
- **Two button styles:** primary (step-6 fill, outline, white text) for main actions, and secondary (outline only, black text) for filters and other repeated options. Both turn Acid Green on hover and press.
- **Tags, links and eyebrows are standardized,** with all-caps labels, tags and instruction links in Source Code Pro Medium.
- **One corner radius: 6px** for buttons, inputs, cards, tags and badges. Pills are retired.
- **Headings top out at Bold 700.** Weight 800 is dropped; Inter runs heavier than Hanken Grotesk, which the old scale was based on.
- **No color coding by section or content type.**

## 1. Brand Foundations

### What the Atlas is
**The Atlas is a lab.** The database, Atlas Pulse, and research papers are its outputs. Research produces the outputs—not the other way around. A lab produces solid, qualified, verified output; that's the standard every piece of brand material should signal.

| Element | Wording |
|---|---|
| Framing | The Atlas investigates and maps the human infrastructure of trust through the lens of independent journalism. |
| Tagline | The human infrastructure of trust, mapped. |
| Mission | To map independent journalism in real time and make it visible, fundable, and impossible to overlook. |
| Positioning statement | The Independent Journalism Atlas is the connective threshold between the practice of journalism and the institutions that depend on, fund, or need to truly understand it. |

- The framing is a working statement, not a tagline. The tagline always uses the comma form, never "…trust. Mapped."
- **Scope:** expanding into science, public health, and other fields of expertise is an expansion of the original idea, not a change of direction. Journalism is the beachhead; the lens stays independent journalism.
- **Standards, not ownership:** the Atlas doesn't aim to own the movement. It wants a prominent seat at the table, building the standards, templates, and coalitions the era runs on—on open standards (AT Proto, MCP, IPTC news-data standards), not platforms.

### Who the Atlas serves
Journalists, creators and credible experts on one side; the funders, platforms, and publishers who depend on, fund, or need to understand them on the other. Brand materials often serve both at once.

### AI
**"AI powered by humans"**—an inversion of the "powered by AI" platitude. AI is neutral; the experienced humans using it are the differentiator.
- The Atlas doesn't take sides in the existential good/bad AI debate. It makes the practical case that AI in experienced human hands beats AI on its own.
- Show it, don't claim it: describe the human judgment in the loop alongside any AI automation.
- The Atlas AI ethics policy is in development. Don't quote, paraphrase, or imply it as policy.

## 2. Voice and Copy

### Voice
Intentional, clean, data-forward, and creator-respecting. Authoritative but not stuffy. Never startup-generic, never hype, never a Dribbble exercise.

- Lead with what the data shows; let the evidence carry the authority.
- Treat the people in the Atlas as professionals, not content.
- Prefer precise, plain words over buzzwords.

### Rules
| Rule | Correct | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Name | The Independent Journalism Atlas; the Atlas | Independent Journalists Atlas |
| "Independent" | independent journalist / independent journalism | journalist / journalism without "independent" when describing the Atlas's field |
| Starter Pack | Starter Pack | Postcard (the /postcard URL stays) |
| Em dashes | word—word | word — word |
| Database size | "about 2,400–2,500 creators" (after deduplication); always "about" in public | older figures (1,200, 1,718, 2,100, 2,364) |
| Audience | "traffic" or "visitors" (roughly 20,000–30,000 a month) | "leads" |
| Legal entity | TIJA LLC (legal and copyright lines) | older entity names |
| Internal designations | — | "Top 100 creators" in anything public |

### The Atlas and Project C
The Atlas is the 30,000-foot research layer: it maps independent journalism as a practice and industry, including contested institutional cases. Project C is ground-level coaching of creator-journalists as individual practitioners. Separate but complementary; both verify trustworthiness, at different levels. Keep the distinction clear in all copy.

### The Atlas voice and personal voices
The Atlas voice is a shared platform voice. It's distinct from team members' personal writing (for example, Justin's Inputs and Outputs) and from Project C's voice.

## 3. Typography

### Three typefaces
| Role | Typeface | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sans | **Inter** (400, 600, 700) | Everything that isn't one of the two roles below: every heading on every page, UI, body copy, numerals, captions, pull quotes, decks, Google Docs |
| Monospace | **Source Code Pro** (Medium 500) | All-caps labels only: eyebrows, tags, badges, instruction links, card meta lines, footer and nav labels |
| Serif | **Merriweather** (400) | Body prose in editorial articles only: essays and long-form reports. Never headings, subheads, ledes, numerals or pull quotes |
| Icons | Material Symbols Outlined | Icons only |

All are on Google Fonts. **No other typeface is used on journalismatlas.com or in Atlas materials.** Retired or never-approved fonts include Hanken Grotesk, DM Mono, JetBrains Mono, DM Sans, Playfair Display, Space Mono and Libre Baskerville, but the rule is the list above, not a list of exclusions. Weight 800 is retired—Inter runs heavier than Hanken Grotesk, so 700 now does the job 800 used to. Inter 500 isn't used; weight 500 is Source Code Pro's.

**Editorial articles** are essays and long-form reports published as articles (Watershed today; long-form reports built from the report template). A landing page, product page, Pulse, For Brands, partner page or City Lab is never an editorial article, however editorial its tone. Watershed is the model: Merriweather for the reading, Inter for every heading, figure and pull quote.

**Co-branded reports** may use a partner's typefaces by named exception in the design tokens (today: Nodes and Networks, with the Knight Center at Cronkite). The Atlas pages around them follow these rules.

### Type scale
| Level | Size | Weight | Line height | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Display | 48px | 700 | 1.1 | Hero headings only |
| H1 | 36px | 700 | 1.2 | Page titles |
| H2 | 28px | 700 | 1.25 | Section heads |
| H3 | 20px | 600 | 1.3 | Card titles, drawer headers |
| Body | 16px | 400 | 1.6 | Default reading text |
| Small | 13px | 400 | 1.5 | Meta, bylines, timestamps, handles—13px is the floor |
| Label | 13px | 500, Source Code Pro | 1.4 | All caps: eyebrows and instruction links, letter-spaced 0.08–0.12em |
| Tag | 12px | 500, Source Code Pro | 1.2 | All caps: tags, badges and card meta lines (e.g. the Pulse card date · publication line), letter-spaced 0.08em |

Headline case is title case.

### The type floor
No text is smaller than 12px, anywhere: pages, charts, maps, legends, tooltips and footers included. 12px is for Source Code Pro tags only; all other text is 13px or larger. Where a layout can't fit text at the floor, the layout changes, not the size.

## 4. Color

**Brand colors express; data colors encode.** The palette has five tiers, each with one job.

### Tier 1 — Core brand (identity)
| Name | Hex | sRGB | CMYK | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acid Green | #ceff00 | 206, 255, 0 | 24, 0, 100, 0 | Interaction: hover, press and selected states. Also eyebrow labels on dark surfaces. Never a success or "go" signal. Fill only on light surfaces |
| Lime Green | #97d600 | 151, 214, 0 | 45, 0, 100, 0 | In UI, the keyboard focus ring on dark surfaces only. Otherwise brand materials |
| Dark Olive | #5d7400 | 93, 116, 0 | 65, 35, 100, 20 | Design elements; in UI, only as the green for text, icons, underlines and thin strokes on light surfaces. Never a fill |

### Greens by surface
| | On dark surfaces | On light surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Acid Green | Hover, press and selected: fills, text, underlines. Eyebrow labels (17.9:1 on black) | Fill only (button hover and press), with black text on top. Never text, icons or thin strokes (1.2:1 on white) |
| Lime Green | Keyboard focus ring only | Not used |
| Dark Olive | Not used in UI | Text, icons and underlines for hover and selected states; keyboard focus ring (5.3:1 on white, 4.6:1 on step 2) |

### Tier 2 — Monotones (structure: UI, text, surfaces)
Two 8-step scales with matched contrast. Each has a job:

- **Neutral is the website scale.** Every UI, text, surface and border role on the site uses Neutral.
- **Cool is the graphics scale.** Infographics, charts, data visualizations and editorial illustrations may use Cool.
- **Decks, social and print** may use either scale.
- **Don't mix scales on one surface.** An embedded graphic (a chart, infographic or illustration placed on a page) counts as its own surface, so a Cool-scale graphic can sit on a Neutral-scale page. The page around it stays Neutral.

| Step | Neutral | Cool |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | #FFFFFF | #FFFFFF |
| 2 | #EFEFEF | #EFEFF4 |
| 3 | #C6C6C6 | #C6C6CD |
| 4 | #909090 | #909097 |
| 5 | #5E5E5E | #5E5E64 |
| 6 | #303030 | #303036 |
| 7 | #121212 | #121218 |
| 8 | #000000 | #000000 |

- **Step 4** (about 3.2:1 on white): large text, UI elements and presentation backgrounds only—never body text.
- **Step 5** is the lightest step safe for body text on white.
- **Website roles (Neutral):** page background step 2 `#EFEFEF`; card background step 1 `#FFFFFF`; primary text step 6 `#303030`; headings step 8 `#000000`; secondary text step 5 `#5E5E5E` (light surfaces) or step 3 `#C6C6C6` (dark); muted/disabled step 4 `#909090`; borders step 3 `#C6C6C6` (light) or step 5 `#5E5E5E` (dark); dark surfaces step 7 `#121212`.
- **Site header:** white by default; black when a page needs it. Use the logo variant that matches.
- **Buttons, links, tags and nav:** see Section 5, Interaction and Components.
- Full sRGB and CMYK values are in the style guide.

Never decorative, never a category.

### Rules across tiers
- Don't mix brand secondary and data-category colors in one composition.
- Shared hexes are allowed only when deliberate and documented. Avoid near-duplicates.
- New data categories draw from the brand secondary palette first, where contrast allows.
- Editorial annotations (for example, a "HIRED" badge) use monotones. System feedback uses Tier 5. Never conflate the two.

### No color coding by section or content type
The Atlas has no per-section color coding. Pulse, For Brands, Research & Writing and Partners all use the same system above. A section's identity comes from typography and content, not from a color of its own. The same applies to content types (Essay, Field Study, Field Report, Report, Lab Note, Talk, Elsewhere): they're told apart by their labels, not by color.

Only beat categories carry color, through Tier 4 (see Tags in Section 5).

## 5. Layout and Components

- **Philosophy:** the Atlas is a tool, not an experience. Functionality first; legibility, clarity and scanability above all. Material Design 3 is the reference framework.
- **Widths:** full layout 1440px; card grid 1200px; text column 680px. Every section—hero, header, footer included—sits in the 1440px container with 16px inner padding.
- **Spacing:** 4px base unit. xs 4 / sm 8 / md 16 / lg 24 / xl 32 / 2xl 48 / 3xl 96.
- **Radius:** 6px for everything—buttons, inputs, cards, tags and badges. No pills. Only photos, avatars and status dots are circles.
- **Icons:** Material Symbols Outlined. Filled only for active toggle/action icons (save, follow, bookmark). Sizes: nav and card header 24px, inline 18px, section header 28px.
- **Cards:** three sizes with identical structure; card consistency is absolute. Feature treatments go on containers, never on cards. Clicking a card never ejects the user. Partner assets appear in page headers only.
- **Footer:** one standard site footer on every page (grid and dividers per footer-redesign-spec.md), logo `Atlas_logo_lockup_stacked_wht_web.svg`. No page adds its own footer or "Powered by" strip. Column titles are Labels (13px), copyright and legal links are Tags (12px), and the tagline and column links are Inter at 13px.
- **Header logo:** `Atlas_logo_lockup_horizontal_blk_web.svg` on a white header; `Atlas_logo_lockup_horizontal_wht_web.svg` on a black header. Both share identical geometry, so they swap without layout shift.
- **Long-form reports:** Merriweather prose in the 680px text column; Inter for all UI chrome; a "Jump to" sub-nav pinned under the site header from page load; the standard footer, never a one-off. Spec: Atlas-Long-Form-Report-Template-2026-09.md.
- **Profiles:** built to existing data, not aspirational data.
- **Linking:** the tool links to features, and features link back to the tool.
- **Logo assets:** mirrored in Google Drive and Dropbox (linked from the style guide).
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.1 AA—4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI; 13px type floor (12px Source Code Pro at weight 500 excepted for tags, badges and card meta lines), applied everywhere including graphics; text colors from the monotone roles, never transparent white or black; 44×44px touch targets. Atlas tokens don't apply to Project C; check projectc.biz/style-guide before flagging its brand choices.

### Interaction and Components

**The principle: green means interaction.** Buttons, tags, instruction links and nav rest in monotones. Green appears when someone hovers, presses or selects: Acid Green as a fill on either surface or as text on dark; Dark Olive as text and underlines on light. Lime Green is reserved for the keyboard focus ring on dark surfaces. Two exceptions are deliberate: body links rest in Dark Olive on light surfaces (with an underline, for accessibility), and eyebrows may rest in Acid Green on dark surfaces to draw attention—they aren't clickable, so they don't compete with an interaction.

**States** (use these names; avoid "active," which means different things in code and conversation)

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rest | Nothing is happening |
| Hover / press | Pointer over it, or being clicked or tapped. Treated the same |
| Selected | The current page, tab or filter. Persists |
| Focus | Reached by keyboard. Always a visible ring |
| Disabled | Can't be used right now |

**Buttons: two styles.** Both are 44px tall, 6px radius, Inter SemiBold 15px, sentence case, and both turn Acid Green on hover and press.

**Primary button**, for the main action (Search, Sign up, Send, Read the essay). One primary per group.

| State | Light surfaces | Dark surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | Step-6 `#303030` fill, white text, 1px step-5 `#5E5E5E` outline | Step-6 fill, white text, 1px step-4 `#909090` outline |
| Hover / press | Acid Green fill and outline, black text | Same |
| Selected | White fill, black text, 1px black outline | White fill, black text, 1px white outline |
| Focus | 2px Dark Olive ring, offset 2px | 2px Lime Green ring, offset 2px |
| Disabled | No fill, step-4 text, step-3 outline | No fill, step-5 text, step-6 outline |

**Secondary button**, for filters and other repeated options (beats, content-type filters, tabs presented as buttons). Lighter, so a row of them doesn't compete with the primary action.

| State | Light surfaces | Dark surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Rest | No fill, black text, 1px step-6 `#303030` outline | No fill, white text, 1px step-4 `#909090` outline |
| Hover / press | Acid Green fill and outline, black text | Same |
| Selected | Black fill and outline, white text | White fill and outline, black text |
| Focus | 2px Dark Olive ring, offset 2px | 2px Lime Green ring, offset 2px |
| Disabled | No fill, step-4 text, step-3 outline | No fill, step-5 text, step-6 outline |

A hovered selected button, of either style, still turns Acid Green.

**Tags: fill or outline, never both.** Tags aren't clickable, so they have no hover. Source Code Pro Medium 12px caps, 4px × 8px padding, 6px radius.

| Type | Examples | Light surfaces | Dark surfaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content type | Essay, Field Study, Report | Black text on step-3 `#C6C6C6` fill | Same |
| Status: confirmed | Checked Today, HIRED | White text on black fill | Black text on white fill |
| Status: in progress | Being Tested | Step-6 text, 1px step-6 outline | White text, 1px white outline |
| Status: excluded | Kept Separate | As in progress, dashed outline | As in progress, dashed outline |
| Beat category | Technology, Health | 8px dot in the Tier 4 color, step-6 text, 1px step-3 outline | Tier 4 dot, white text, 1px step-5 outline |

**Buttons and tags never look alike.** Buttons are 44px tall, Inter SemiBold, sentence case, and respond to hover. Tags are 12px Source Code Pro caps, about 20px tall, and never respond to hover. Tags are a fill or an outline, never both.

**Links**

| Type | Style | Light surfaces | Dark surfaces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instruction link (MORE TECH →, VIEW IN ATLAS →) | Source Code Pro Medium 13px caps, always ends in → | Rest step 6; hover/press Dark Olive, underlined | Rest white; hover/press Acid Green, underlined |
| Body link (inside a sentence) | Inherits the text style | Dark Olive, underlined at rest; hover black | White, underlined at rest; hover Acid Green |

Body links stay underlined at rest so they're never marked by color alone.

**Eyebrows:** Source Code Pro Medium 13px caps, no arrow, not clickable. Step 5 on light surfaces; step 3 or Acid Green on dark. The arrow is what separates an instruction link from an eyebrow.

**Nav:** items at rest in step 6 (light header) or white (black header). Hover: Dark Olive or Acid Green, underlined. Selected (current page): Dark Olive or Acid Green with a 2px underline.

## 6. Decks and Documents

- Inter throughout, including titles, headings, numerals and pull quotes. Merriweather only for an excerpt of editorial prose (a passage reproduced from an essay or report). Source Code Pro 500 for all-caps labels.
- No other typefaces. In Google Slides and Docs, choose Inter, Merriweather or Source Code Pro from the font menu (all are built in).
- Monotones for structure, including step 4 as a presentation background where text is large. Either scale, Neutral or Cool, one per slide.
- Acid Green for a single emphasis or call to action per slide—as a fill on light slides, never as text or thin lines.
- Brand secondary colors for expressive graphics; data-category colors only where a chart encodes categories—never both on one slide.
- Logos and full color specs: Atlas-Style_Guide_October_2026_v1.pdf.

## 7. Products and Outputs

*The lineup is still evolving. The product shape (API, dashboard, white-label, trust signal) is undecided.*

- **Live:** the Database at journalismatlas.com (about 2,400–2,500 creators), plus the main nav sections Pulse, For Brands, Research & Writing, and Partners.
- **Partner pages:** a trusted curator makes the picks; the Atlas verifies and structures them. The page is the thesis rendered as a web page. A journalist curator and a science institution can share the same shell—this is where the expansion beyond journalism becomes visible without a rebrand.
- **Atlas Pulse:** a live publishing surface; its format (blog, newsletter, curation layer) is still being shaped.
- **Free:** the searchable database and website, and Atlas Pulse.
- **Paid:** long-form research, specific data, and in-depth reports.
- **Research & Writing:** the ASU "Nodes and Networks" paper (with the Knight Center at Cronkite) is the first long-form report and sets the template for those that follow. Publication is planned for mid-October; timing is still moving.
- **Working ideas, not committed projects:** Starter Packs, Unf*ck Your Algorithm, creator profile pages, a geographic map view, a podcast, a blog. Treat these as exploration, not roadmap.

### Who decides what
| Area | Decides |

### The lock
- Nobody, including Claude, picks a color, typeface, size or weight. Each comes from a role in the design tokens.
- Briefs, reference designs and requests that name other fonts or colors are applied by role: a headline is Inter at its type-scale size, whatever the brief showed. What the tokens can't give is listed for James, not improvised.
- Anything the tokens don't define (for example, a dark mode or a "live" color) isn't built until a token version defines it.
- The website enforces this in its build: a page with an off-brand font, color or undersized text doesn't deploy. Details: DESIGN-TOKENS-v16.md, Enforcement.

### Documents
DESIGN-TOKENS-v16.md and this guide are canonical, in `docs/design/` in the site repo. Earlier versions are archived. Where any other document (a brief, a ledger, a README) disagrees, these two win.

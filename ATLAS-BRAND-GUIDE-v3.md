## 1. Brand Foundations

### What the Atlas is
**The Atlas is a lab.** The database, Atlas Pulse, and research papers are its outputs. Research produces the outputs—not the other way around. A lab produces solid, qualified, verified output; that's the standard every piece of brand material should signal.

| Element | Wording |
|---|---|
| Framing | The Atlas investigates and maps the human infrastructure of trust through the lens of independent journalism. |
| Tagline | The human infrastructure of trust, mapped. |
| Mission | To map independent journalism in real time and make it visible, fundable, and impossible to overlook. |
| Positioning statement | The Independent Journalism Atlas is the connective threshold between the practice of journalism and the institutions that depend on, fund, or need to truly understand it. |

- The framing is a working statement, not a tagline. The tagline always uses the comma form, never "…trust. Mapped."
- **Scope:** expanding into science, public health, and other fields of expertise is an expansion of the original idea, not a change of direction. Journalism is the beachhead; the lens stays independent journalism.
- **Standards, not ownership:** the Atlas doesn't aim to own the movement. It wants a prominent seat at the table, building the standards, templates, and coalitions the era runs on—on open standards (AT Proto, MCP, IPTC news-data standards), not platforms.

### Who the Atlas serves
Journalists, creators and credible experts on one side; the funders, platforms, and publishers who depend on, fund, or need to understand them on the other. Brand materials often serve both at once.

### AI
**"AI powered by humans"**—an inversion of the "powered by AI" platitude. AI is neutral; the experienced humans using it are the differentiator.
- The Atlas doesn't take sides in the existential good/bad AI debate. It makes the practical case that AI in experienced human hands beats AI on its own.
- Show it, don't claim it: describe the human judgment in the loop alongside any AI automation.
- The Atlas AI ethics policy is in development. Don't quote, paraphrase, or imply it as policy.

## 2. Voice and Copy

### Voice
Intentional, clean, data-forward, and creator-respecting. Authoritative but not stuffy. Never startup-generic, never hype, never a Dribbble exercise.

- Lead with what the data shows; let the evidence carry the authority.
- Treat the people in the Atlas as professionals, not content.
- Prefer precise, plain words over buzzwords.

### Rules
| Rule | Correct | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Name | The Independent Journalism Atlas; the Atlas | Independent Journalists Atlas |
| "Independent" | independent journalist / independent journalism | journalist / journalism without "independent" when describing the Atlas's field |
| Starter Pack | Starter Pack | Postcard (the /postcard URL stays) |
| Em dashes | word—word | word — word |
| Database size | the build-time count with its as-of date (e.g. "2,415 verified creator-journalists, as of October 25, 2026"); never typed by hand, see CLAUDE.md "Creator Count Convention" | hand-typed counts, rounded forms ("1,800+", "about 2,400–2,500"), older figures (1,200, 1,718, 2,100, 2,364) |
| Audience | "traffic" or "visitors" (roughly 20,000–30,000 a month) | "leads" |
| Legal entity | TIJA LLC (legal and copyright lines) | older entity names |
| Internal designations | — | "Top 100 creators" in anything public |

### The Atlas and Project C
The Atlas is the 30,000-foot research layer: it maps independent journalism as a practice and industry, including contested institutional cases. Project C is ground-level coaching of creator-journalists as individual practitioners. Separate but complementary; both verify trustworthiness, at different levels. Keep the distinction clear in all copy.

### The Atlas voice and personal voices
The Atlas voice is a shared platform voice. It's distinct from team members' personal writing (for example, Justin's Inputs and Outputs) and from Project C's voice.

## 4. Color

**Brand colors express; data colors encode.** The palette has five tiers, each with one job.

### Tier 1 — Core brand (identity)
| Name | Hex | sRGB | CMYK | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acid Green | #ceff00 | 206, 255, 0 | 24, 0, 100, 0 | CTAs and active states only; one per composition; never a success or "go" signal. Fill only on light surfaces |
| Lime Green | #97d600 | 151, 214, 0 | 45, 0, 100, 0 | Hover only. Fill only on light surfaces |
| Dark Olive | #5d7400 | 93, 116, 0 | 65, 35, 100, 20 | Design elements; in UI, only as the green for text, icons and thin strokes on light surfaces |

### Greens by surface
| | On dark surfaces | On light surfaces |
|---|---|---|
| Acid Green | Text, icons, strokes and fills (17.9:1 on black) | Fill only, with black or step-6 text on top. Never text, icons or thin strokes (1.2:1 on white) |
| Lime Green | Hover; focus ring | Fill only. Never text, icons or thin strokes (1.8:1 on white) |
| Dark Olive | Not used in UI | Text, icons and thin strokes where green carries meaning: active, selected, link-style CTA, focus ring (5.3:1 on white) |

### Tier 2 — Monotones (structure: UI, text, surfaces)
Two 8-step scales, interchangeable for brand materials. **The website uses the Cool scale.** Don't mix scales on one surface.

| Step | Neutral | Cool |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | #FFFFFF | #FFFFFF |
| 2 | #EFEFEF | #EFEFF4 |
| 3 | #C6C6C6 | #C6C6CD |
| 4 | #909090 | #909097 |
| 5 | #5E5E5E | #5E5E64 |
| 6 | #303030 | #303036 |
| 7 | #121212 | #121218 |
| 8 | #000000 | #000000 |

- **Step 4** (about 3.2:1 on white): large text, UI elements and presentation backgrounds only—never body text.
- **Step 5** is the lightest step safe for body text on white.
- **Website roles:** page background step 2; primary text step 6; secondary text step 5 (light surfaces) or step 3 (dark); muted/disabled step 4; borders step 3 (light) or step 5 (dark).
- **Site header:** white by default; black when a page needs it. Use the logo variant that matches.
- **Primary CTA:** acid fill with black text at rest and active; lime fill with black text on hover; focus ring Dark Olive on light surfaces, lime on dark.
- **Inline links:** Dark Olive, underlined, on light surfaces; white, underlined, on dark.
- Full sRGB and CMYK values are in the style guide.

| Role | Value |
|---|---|
| Error | #f50000 |

Never decorative, never a category.

### Rules across tiers
- Don't mix brand secondary and data-category colors in one composition.
- Shared hexes are allowed only when deliberate and documented. Avoid near-duplicates.
- New data categories draw from the brand secondary palette first, where contrast allows.
- Editorial annotations (for example, a "HIRED" badge) use monotones. System feedback uses Tier 5. Never conflate the two.

## 5. Layout and Components

- **Philosophy:** the Atlas is a tool, not an experience. Functionality first; legibility, clarity and scanability above all. Material Design 3 is the reference framework.
- **Widths:** full layout 1440px; card grid 1200px; text column 680px. Every section—hero, header, footer included—sits in the 1440px container with 16px inner padding.
- **Spacing:** 4px base unit. xs 4 / sm 8 / md 16 / lg 24 / xl 32 / 2xl 48 / 3xl 96.
- **Radius:** 6px cards and buttons; 9999px pills and tags.
- **Icons:** Material Symbols Outlined. Filled only for active toggle/action icons (save, follow, bookmark). Sizes: nav and card header 24px, inline 18px, section header 28px.
- **Cards:** three sizes with identical structure; card consistency is absolute. Feature treatments go on containers, never on cards. Clicking a card never ejects the user. Partner assets appear in page headers only.
- **Footer:** one standard site footer everywhere (grid and dividers per footer-redesign-spec.md), logo `Atlas_logo_lockup_stacked_wht_web.svg`.
- **Header logo:** `Atlas_logo_lockup_horizontal_blk_web.svg` on a white header; `Atlas_logo_lockup_horizontal_wht_web.svg` on a black header. Both share identical geometry, so they swap without layout shift.
- **Long-form reports:** Merriweather prose in the 680px text column; Inter for all UI chrome; a "Jump to" sub-nav pinned under the site header from page load; the standard footer, never a one-off. Spec: Atlas-Long-Form-Report-Template-2026-09.md.
- **Profiles:** built to existing data, not aspirational data.
- **Linking:** the tool links to features, and features link back to the tool.
- **Logo assets:** mirrored in Google Drive and Dropbox (linked from the style guide).
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.1 AA—4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI; 13px type floor (11px Micro labels at weight 500 excepted); 44×44px touch targets. Atlas tokens don't apply to Project C; check projectc.biz/style-guide before flagging its brand choices.

## 6. Decks and Documents

- Inter throughout; Merriweather for quotes and editorial passages.
- Monotones for structure, including step 4 as a presentation background where text is large.
- Acid Green for a single emphasis or call to action per slide—as a fill on light slides, never as text or thin lines.
- Brand secondary colors for expressive graphics; data-category colors only where a chart encodes categories—never both on one slide.
- Logos and full color specs: Atlas-Style_Guide_October_2026_v1.pdf.

## 7. Products and Outputs

*The lineup is still evolving. The product shape (API, dashboard, white-label, trust signal) is undecided.*

- **Live:** the Database at journalismatlas.com (about 2,400–2,500 creators), plus the main nav sections Pulse, For Brands, Research & Writing, and Partners.
- **Partner pages:** a trusted curator makes the picks; the Atlas verifies and structures them. The page is the thesis rendered as a web page. A journalist curator and a science institution can share the same shell—this is where the expansion beyond journalism becomes visible without a rebrand.
- **Atlas Pulse:** a live publishing surface; its format (blog, newsletter, curation layer) is still being shaped.
- **Free:** the searchable database and website, and Atlas Pulse.
- **Paid:** long-form research, specific data, and in-depth reports.
- **Research & Writing:** the ASU "Nodes and Networks" paper (with the Knight Center at Cronkite) is the first long-form report and sets the template for those that follow. Publication is planned for mid-October; timing is still moving.
- **Working ideas, not committed projects:** Starter Packs, Unf*ck Your Algorithm, creator profile pages, a geographic map view, a podcast, a blog. Treat these as exploration, not roadmap.

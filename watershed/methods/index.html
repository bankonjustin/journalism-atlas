How we did this

What this is. "A Watershed Moment for News Deserts" is a reported essay, not a census. It names places and people to show a pattern we keep seeing. It doesn't claim to measure how common that pattern is. We're working on that, and we'll publish it when we can stand behind a number.

Medill's data. On Oct. 13, 2026, we pulled the county-level data behind Medill's State of Local News 2026 explorer and reproduced its count of 238 counties with no locally based source of news. When the essay says a place is or isn't in Medill's count, that's from this data. We didn't change or correct Medill's figures. Where something puzzled us, we've shared it with Medill's team rather than guess.

The Independent Journalism Atlas. The Atlas maps independent creators doing journalism's work: reporting, analysis, curation and explanation, on channels they own. To be included, a creator has to be independent and identifiable, have a real publication or channel, show sustained, recent work, and serve an audience's need to know. Entries are reviewed against our editorial standards. As of October 2026, the Atlas holds more than 2,400 creators.

How we looked. Between Oct. 13 and Oct. 22, we searched in and around the places in this essay and dozens of other counties on Medill's list. We started from people instead of outlets: who posts about the county board, who covers the high school, who records the meetings, who people in neighboring towns follow. We searched public websites and newsletter platforms, and we read social platforms the way a resident would. When we used a signed-in account, a person watched the session, and nothing was posted, liked or followed.

What we couldn't see. Much of the local conversation in these places happens in private Facebook groups, in print and on the radio. We didn't join private groups or read their posts. "We didn't find it" means our methods didn't find it, not that it isn't there.

The figures. County counts come from Medill's 2026 data. Atlas totals are as of the dates shown. The surface chart counts automated local-news items and pieces of local journalism about each county from Sept. 1 to mid-October 2026; those counts are lower bounds. The grid of 40 squares shows the desert counties our national sweep checked. The price chart draws on the News Creator Corps fellowship terms, Project C's analysis of the Sebastopol Times and Gao, Lee and Murphy in the Journal of Financial Economics (2020), with the working paper at Brookings.

How we used AI. AI research agents did much of the searching and sorting: running searches, logging what they found and when it was posted, and flagging candidates. People decided who counts, opened every source the essay relies on, and checked every fact. We also used AI tools for editing help and to prepare fact checks. The reporting choices, the arguments and the words are ours.

The art. Illustrations are by James Bareham, drawn by hand.
